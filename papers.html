Papers

Yes, there are academic papers about INTERCAL. No, we are not joking. These are real results with real proofs. The language just happens to be absurd.

COME FROM Considered Helpful

Jason Whittington and Claude (Anthropic), 2026

The COME FROM statement has been INTERCAL's most ridiculed feature for over thirty years. This paper proves it is computationally necessary.

Main result: INTERCAL-72's NEXT/RESUME control flow cannot express callable subroutines containing arbitrary-length loops. The NEXT stack imposes a hard limit of 79 iterations. COME FROM removes this limitation entirely.

The proof uses TLA+ model checking to verify that no arrangement of NEXT, RESUME, and FORGET can implement a general loop within a callable subroutine without corrupting the NEXT stack. The paper then demonstrates that COME FROM loops — combined with the double-NEXT trampoline pattern — provide stack-free iteration with preserved return addresses.

Key Results

  • Lemma 1: FORGET in a callable subroutine destroys the caller's return address. A subroutine that loops via NEXT/FORGET cannot return correctly.
  • Lemma 2: NEXT/RESUME loops accumulate residual stack entries. After 79 iterations, E123 (NEXT stack overflow) terminates the program.
  • Theorem: No arrangement of INTERCAL-72 control flow statements can implement a callable subroutine containing an arbitrary-length loop.
  • Corollary: Bubble sort is limited to arrays of 13 elements. 13 x 12 / 2 = 78 ≤ 79.

The paper is a deliberate riff on Dijkstra's "Go To Statement Considered Harmful" (1968), arguing that the statement Dijkstra would have despised most is the one INTERCAL actually needs.

SIGBOVIK 2026 Submission

Jason Whittington and Claude (Anthropic), 2026

A condensed version of the COME FROM proof submitted to SIGBOVIK, the annual conference for research that is technically correct but probably shouldn't exist. The submission includes the TLA+ model, the 15-line Lemma 1 reproducer, and cross-validation results on both SCHRODIE and C-INTERCAL.
